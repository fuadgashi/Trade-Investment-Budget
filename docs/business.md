# The business

[← README](../README.md)

A distributor buys fast-moving consumer goods from brand owners and sells them to shops,
restaurants and other customers. To win shelf space and volume it invests in its customers:
an annual bonus on what they buy, secondary placements in the store, campaigns, promotions,
credit notes and extra discounts. That is **trade investment**.

The distributor funds it from the price. Each brand's selling price is built to set aside a
share of the net sales for each kind of spend; suppliers add to the pot with credit notes and
by paying for services. The model answers four questions:

1. **What does the price set aside, product by product?** The selling price built up from
   cost, distribution and the trade rates, and what is left for marketing — see
   [trade-investment.md](trade-investment.md#the-price-calculation).
2. **Does the bonus and position spend stay within what the price set aside?** In total, and
   customer by customer — see [trade-investment.md](trade-investment.md#annual-bonus) and
   [trade-investment.md](trade-investment.md#secondary-positions).
3. **Is the marketing budget in balance?** What is accumulated against campaigns,
   promotions, discounts and credit notes — see
   [trade-investment.md](trade-investment.md#marketing-budget).
4. **What does trade investment cost in all?** Every pot side by side — see
   [trade-investment.md](trade-investment.md#total-trade-investment).

## Customers and outlets

- A **customer** is the business partner that is invoiced; an **outlet** is one of its points
  of sale. Sales and trade terms are agreed and recorded per outlet; credit notes, service
  invoices and the customer lists on the pages are per customer.
- Every outlet has a **channel**, a **region** and a **city**, kept in the source; the channel
  slicer reads them.

## Brands, divisions and products

- A **product** is one catalogue item, counted in its base unit, with its packing.
- Its **brand** carries the four rates the price sets aside; brands are grouped in commercial
  **divisions**.
- **Goods only.** Service and fixed-asset lines never reach a sales or cost figure, so cost,
  price and the rates sit on one base.

## Where the money comes from

| Pot | Accumulated by | Spent on |
|---|---|---|
| Annual bonus | the bonus rate in the price | the customers' bonus terms |
| Secondary positions | the position rate in the price | placements in the stores |
| Marketing | what the price leaves after cost and the four rates, credit notes received, service invoices to suppliers | campaigns, promotions, seasonal placements, extra discounts, credit notes issued |
